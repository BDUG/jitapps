# TLAL OS style guide

Provided by the user. Master logo artwork was not included.

The visual identity combines circular forms with directional geometry, creating a language that suggests:

Systems — connected parts working as one
Motion — continuous progress rather than static structure
Direction — a clear path forward
Coordination — multiple elements organized around a common center
Precision — deliberate, functional, and engineered
Humanity — warm colors balance the technical nature of the system
TLAL OS should communicate:

Clarity without sterility. Technology without coldness. Momentum without chaos.

2. Logo
TLAL LOgo

The primary TLAL OS identity consists of a circular, multi-part symbol paired with the TLAL OS wordmark.

The symbol uses three principal colors:

Maroon
Orange
Blue
The wordmark uses a dark neutral.

Whenever possible, use the original logo artwork rather than reconstructing the mark.

Logo Character
The circular architecture is a central part of the identity.

Its segmented construction communicates a system made from coordinated components, while its directional geometry introduces movement and progression.

The logo should feel:

Structured. Dynamic. Precise. Confident.

3. Logo Usage
Clear Space
Always give the TLAL OS logo enough room to remain visually distinct.

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least the approximate height of the OS letterforms in the wordmark.

No text, imagery, interface elements, borders, or other logos should enter this area.

Use additional space for prominent brand applications whenever possible.

Minimum Size
Recommended minimum sizes:

Digital
Full logo: 120px wide
Symbol alone: 32px wide
Print
Full logo: 30mm wide
Symbol alone: 10mm wide
At very small sizes, use the standalone symbol rather than forcing the complete wordmark into insufficient space.

Approved Treatments
Prefer:

Full-color logo on a white or light background
Full-color symbol with dark wordmark
Single-color dark version when color reproduction is unavailable
White/reversed version on sufficiently dark backgrounds
The full-color version should be the default expression of the brand.

Incorrect Usage
Do not:

Stretch or compress the logo
Rotate the logo
Rearrange individual components
Recolor components arbitrarily
Replace brand colors with gradients
Add drop shadows, bevels, glows, or outlines
Place the logo over visually noisy imagery
Change the proportions of the symbol and wordmark
Recreate the logo using substitute shapes
Place the full-color logo on backgrounds that reduce contrast
Use the supplied master artwork whenever possible.

6. Color Hierarchy
A typical TLAL OS composition should prioritize colors approximately in this order:

Neutral → Maroon → Blue → Orange

Think of the palette as a system rather than four equally competing colors.

Neutrals create space. Maroon establishes the brand. Blue structures information. Orange creates energy.

Avoid using maroon, orange, and blue at equal visual intensity across an entire interface or composition.

8. Typography Principles
TLAL OS typography should be:

Direct
Highly legible
Spacious
Structured
Unornamented
Avoid overly decorative typefaces.

Avoid excessive uppercase text.

Use typography and weight to establish hierarchy instead of relying on multiple colors.

Keep line lengths comfortable for reading, particularly in documentation and product interfaces.

10. Graphic Language
TLAL OS graphics should feel systematic rather than decorative.

Recommended graphic devices include:

Circular diagrams
Partial rings
Radial systems
Connected nodes
Directional paths
Progress arcs
Modular grids
Large cropped sections of circular geometry
Use generous negative space around these elements.

Avoid filling every available area with brand graphics.

11. Iconography
Icons should use a consistent geometric style.

Recommended characteristics:

Simple construction
Rounded or subtly softened geometry
Consistent stroke weight
Minimal internal detail
Recognizable at small sizes
Prefer outline icons for general UI.

Use filled icons selectively for active or high-priority states.

Do not mix unrelated icon families.

12. Photography

Real
Contemporary
Purposeful
Human
Active
Naturally composed
Favor images showing people doing, building, moving, coordinating, or interacting rather than generic posed corporate photography.

Avoid:

Artificially staged business imagery
Heavy filters
Excessive saturation
Generic futuristic technology imagery
Glowing digital globes
Random circuit-board graphics
Other common technology clichés
13. Layout
TLAL OS layouts should reflect the underlying idea of an operating system:

Organized complexity.

Use:

Strong grids
Clear alignment
Generous whitespace
Modular sections
Distinct information hierarchy
Predictable spacing
A user should understand the structure of a TLAL OS page before reading every word.

15. UI Direction
TLAL OS interfaces should feel calm and operational.

Surfaces
Prefer:

White
Off-white
Very light neutral gray
Use dark surfaces selectively for:

Navigation
Command-oriented environments
Data-heavy views
High-focus experiences
Primary Action
Use TLAL Maroon where sufficient contrast is maintained.

Secondary Action
Use neutral or outlined treatments.

Informational Action
TLAL Blue can be used when an action is navigational or informational.

Accent
Use TLAL Orange sparingly.

Avoid placing multiple highly saturated button colors beside one another unless their semantic difference is essential.

17. Motion
Motion should reinforce the circular and directional logic of the identity.

Recommended behaviors:

Rotational reveals
Arc progression
Radial expansion
Directional transitions
Modular assembly
Smooth system-state changes
Motion should feel controlled and purposeful, not playful for its own sake.

Avoid excessive bouncing, elastic effects, or ornamental animation.

18. Brand Voice
TLAL OS should communicate like a capable system with a human point of view.

Clear
Say exactly what something does.

Confident
Avoid unnecessary qualifiers and inflated claims.

Intelligent
Explain complex ideas without making them unnecessarily complicated.

Useful
Prioritize information that helps someone make a decision or take action.

Human
Technical precision should never become robotic communication.

19. Writing Principles
Prefer:

Connect your tools in one place.

Instead of:

Leverage our next-generation integrated technology ecosystem.

Prefer:

Three items need your attention.

Instead of:

There are currently three outstanding actionable items requiring user intervention.

Prefer:

Your system is ready.

Instead of:

The initialization procedure has successfully reached completion.

TLAL OS writing should be:

Short when possible. Detailed when necessary. Specific at all times.

20. Naming
Write the brand name consistently as:

TLAL OS

Avoid:

Tlal OS
TLALOS
TLAL-OS
TlalOS
tlal os
Use TLAL OS in prose, titles, interfaces, and external communications unless a technical naming convention requires otherwise.

21. Brand Expression
Every TLAL OS touchpoint should balance three qualities.

Systematic
Information feels organized and intentional.

Dynamic
The brand communicates progress and movement.

Human
Warmth prevents the system from feeling mechanical or inaccessible.

A useful internal test is:

Does this make complexity feel easier to navigate?

If the answer is yes, the execution is likely aligned with TLAL OS.
